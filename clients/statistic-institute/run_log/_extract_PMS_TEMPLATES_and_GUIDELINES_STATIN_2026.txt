GLOSSARY
TERM
DEFINITION
Competency
Observable behaviours that contribute to successful job performance.
Competency Framework
A structured set of competencies required for success in specific roles within the organization.
Goal Setting
The process of establishing clear, measurable objectives aligned with organizational priorities.
Individual Work Plan (IWP)
How each individual will work towards unit objectives.  It includes performance measures and targets (indicators) that indicate task completion or success.
Initiative/Major Activity
Indicate the major projects, or programmes, or policies etc. planned for implementation during the period. These should support, and be aligned to the sub-programme in the Strategic Plan.
Key Outputs
The first level results that an MDA seeks to achieve through the implementation of the various initiatives associated with the respective sub-programmes. Outputs are the products and services produced from interventions (projects, programmes and policies etc.).
The products or services (the results) produced from an individual team member's major tasks/activity.  Usually outlined in the output-focused Job Description.
Operational
Plan
An entity's goals and objectives for any given financial year.  It outlines the major activities or initiatives being undertaken to support the respective sub-programmes and should be aligned to the Strategic Plan.
The strategies associated with the strategic objectives, as outlined in the Strategic Plan, should be translated to the Operational Plan, setting out the MDA's major activities and initiatives, and the outputs, performance measures/indicators, targets and costs associated with each.
The Operational Plan can include other initiatives and activities that are not represented in the strategic plan, that are considered important in the daily operations of the organisation and contribute to the achievement of key strategic objectives.
Outcomes
The desired results that an organisation aims to achieve under each priority. They express the changes or benefits that will be achieved if the initiatives and approaches are implemented successfully.
Performance Appraisal
A formal assessment of an employee's performance against the intended level of performance - clearly defined expectations (outputs, targets), over a specified period.
Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)
A structured plan to help an employee improve performance where expectations are not being met.
Performance
Measure
These are quantifiable measures used to gauge performance. It should state what will be measured to determine the successful completion of the output (i.e. quantity, quality and time).
Performance
Target
The intended level of performance that is to be achieved and can be measured within a specified period of time. Should be aligned to the entity's strategic objectives.  Clearly defined performance expectations.
Strategic
Objectives
Strategic Objectives set the major benchmarks for success and are designed to be measurable, specific and realistic translations of the mission statement and outcomes that can be used by management to guide decision-making.
Strategy
A Plan of Action to achieve strategic objectives.  Strategies combined comprise the Strategic Plan.
Work Plan/Action Plan
How a particular division or unit of an entity will operationalize the entity's Annual Operational Plan
OVERVIEW - STATIN PERFORMANCE MANAGEMENT TEMPLATES GUIDELINES
The successful implementation of STATIN's Performance Management System depends on the consistent and effective use of its supporting tools and templates. The Performance Management System is an important tool for aligning individual performance with organizational goals, clarifying expectations, promoting accountability, and supporting employee development and continuous improvement. Consistent application of the PMS process will contribute to greater transparency, fairness, and effectiveness in managing and improving performance across the organization.
This Guide has been developed to support managers, supervisors, and employees in the accurate and consistent application of the Performance Management System (PMS) templates.  The guidelines provide information to support the various templates and forms utilised in the Implementation process. It provides practical instructions, explanations, and examples to assist users in completing each section of the PMS templates with confidence and clarity.
They describe the proper utilisation of each template and any supporting information or instruction that may be needed. Included in the guidelines are short descriptions of key techniques or terminologies that are useful in educating/orienting the user.
Additionally, the Guidelines provide detailed steps to be taken when implementing the EPM Policy and its Minimum Standards. It guides users at all levels, of the steps to be taken in the performance management and evaluation process. The Policy Guidelines covers all the applicable templates and standard operating procedures in keeping with the EPM Policy and Minimum Standards.
The Guidelines also explain how outstanding performance and underperformance are handled.
In the case of outstanding performances, this leads to the manager or supervisor making recommendation(s) for performance incentive payments.
In cases of underperformance where one achieves a score of less than seventy five percent (75%), then the Guidelines explain the appropriate approach for underperformance.

AGREEING EMPLOYEE PERFORMANCE TARGETS AND KEY OUTPUTS
A Performance Target is the intended level of performance that is to be achieved and can be measured within a specified period of time. These targets should be aligned to STATIN's strategic objectives.
Key Outputs, which are outlined in the employee's job description, are the products or services (the results) produced from major tasks/activities.
In agreeing performance targets and key outputs, the manager and his or her direct report/s will use the Employee Performance Management Form by completing Sections 1-4 and affixing signature immediately after Section 4 in the last quarter of the previous financial year.
This Form will function as the employee's individual work plan and will be used to assess performance at the end of the financial year.
5.1 Responsibilities
The Manager is responsible for developing performance targets for each employee under their supervision as per Unit Plans for each financial year, as per established timelines.
5.2 Forms/Templates

ASSESSING EMPLOYEE PERFORMANCE
Assessing employee performance is a critical component of any performance management system as it helps to ensure a results-oriented organisation. The benefits to be derived include employee growth and development, improved performance and a rewarding work environment.
Best practice dictates that regular assessment of employee performance leads to a high performing organisation. A minimum of three (3) formal performance review conversations using a performance coaching approach should be conducted, and a final assessment at the end of the financial year supported by a minimum of one coaching conversation each quarter.
6.1 Responsibilities
Manager is responsible for assessing performance of each employee under their supervision inclusive of formal performance review conversations
Employees responsible for completing self-assessment of their performance
6.2 Forms/Templates
Employee Performance Management Form (above)

the financial year.

6.3Underperformance:
Where the employee's final score is less than seventy-five (75%) percent, this would indicate that the employee has underperformed in the execution of his/her duties.
Where the employee's underperformance is due to:
the absence or lack of required competences, he/she should be supported with training and development.
their unwillingness to execute agreed work functions/targets, this shall be addressed in keeping with STATIN's policies and procedures.
situations for which s/he has no control (extended illness, study leave, jury duty, national duty and unavailability of organisational resources), the manager and organisation will consider prorating and or modifying performance targets, where the timing of these occurrences can be reasonably considered as a direct impediment to the achievement of performance.
THE PERFORMANCE MANAGEMENT APPEALS PROCESS
An employee who is dissatisfied with the outcome of his/her performance management review has the right to appeal. The steps below outline the Performance Management Appeals Process.
Table 7.1 - Steps in the Performance Management Appeals Process
Steps
Activities
Step 1:
Employee should indicate the problem and basis of the dissatisfaction of his/her performance management review on the relevant section of the Employee Performance Management Form, providing evidence to substantiate.
Step 2:
The employee and manager/supervisor should try to resolve the area(s) defined as problematic through dialogue and an examination of the documentary evidence.
Step 3:
Should the problem remain unresolved the Reviewing Manager will formally enlist the support of HR
Step 4:
Human Resource shall respond in keeping with their internal Appeals/Grievance Process
Step 5:
Should the aggrieved employee remain dissatisfied by the outcome of the Appeal/Grievance process, he/she may pursue recourse in the grievance procedures as outlined in the Labour Relations Industrial Dispute Act.
