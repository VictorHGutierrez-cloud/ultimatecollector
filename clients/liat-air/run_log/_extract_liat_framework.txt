1. Executive Summary & Purpose 
This document is the governing internal instrument for Liat's HRIS evaluation and eventual 
implementation. It consolidates the module-level requirements already developed — Performance 
Management (Balanced Scorecard and 9-Box), Leave Module administrative-access controls, and 
the Staff Travel functional specification — into a single, prioritised set of business requirements, 
one governance structure, one risk register and one evaluation framework, expressed 
independently of any vendor's platform or terminology. 
Two workstreams are grounded in named source material rather than general description. Section 
6 (Performance Management) maps Liat's design directly to the Balanced Scorecard Institute's 
framework and methodology, expressed as system capabilities any qualifying platform must 
deliver. Section 7 (Staff Travel Automation) sets out a platform-agnostic design for employees to 
register and apply for travel, an entitlement ledger that draws down against each employee's 
policy-defined travel benefit as tickets are issued, visibility and an advisory role for Commercial on 
requests that touch seat inventory, and a duty-travel approval chain that puts final authorisation 
with HR (policy compliance) and Finance (budget), consistent with the Staff Travel Policy's own 
approving-officer structure. 
This document is issued within the agreed three-month commercial evaluation window, during 
which platform capability — of the incumbent and any comparator — is being assessed against 
Liat's operational requirements before a Retain / Extend / Replace decision is taken to the Head of 
HR, CFO and CEO. It reflects HR best practice: requirements are sequenced by compliance 
criticality and operational risk rather than vendor convenience, administrative access is controlled 
by segregation-of-duties principles, and every requirement carries a named accountable owner and 
a measurable acceptance test. 
Programme Objectives 
• Establish a single HRIS as the system of record for employee data, leave, performance and 
staff travel, with clearly bounded interfaces to Finance/Xandapay, HITIT and CPAT — 
regardless of which vendor is ultimately selected. 
• Close the two CFO-approved leave policy changes (vacation tiering, sick leave cycle 
migration), consolidate to one policy governing leave, and complete the Xandapay payroll 
integration. 
• Deploy a defensible, audit-ready Performance Management capability built on the Balanced 
Scorecard four-perspective model, weighted competencies, and a 9-box talent matrix (Section 
6). 
• Deploy a staff travel platform covering registration, application, entitlement drawdown, 
Commercial visibility and a duty-travel approval chain authorised by HR and Finance, in line 
with the approved Staff Travel Policy (Section 7). 
• Implement named-user, least-privilege administrative access with logging and periodic review 
— not open-ended “master admin” access, on whichever platform is selected. 
• Obtain and evaluate documented technical, security, hosting, service-level and data-
processing evidence from every vendor under consideration before any Retain / Extend / 
Replace decision is finalised (Section 5).

4. Security, Data Governance & Administrative Access Controls 
(Internal Controls) 
Master/administrative system access shall be provisioned on a controlled, auditable basis on 
whichever platform is selected. Broad administrative rights are not, by themselves, a governance 
control — the controls below are mandatory conditions of any elevated access grant, and apply 
equally to the incumbent and to any replacement platform. 
• Named-user accounts only; no shared or generic administrative credentials. 
• Multi-factor authentication on all administrative accounts, where the platform supports it — 
platform MFA support is itself a mandatory evaluation criterion (Section 5).

• Least-privilege and segregation-of-duties design — no single administrator combines 
configuration, approval and record-purge rights without independent review. 
• Full administrative activity logging (system audit trail), with periodic (at minimum quarterly) 
access reviews. 
• Immediate access revocation on role change or separation, tracked via a documented 
offboarding checklist. 
• Documented approval prior to any sensitive configuration change (rate tables, entitlement 
rules, approval workflows, leave-policy parameters, staff travel entitlement tiers). 
• Data deletion, record purging, or other irreversible database operations require documented 
authorisation, confirmed retention compliance, a verified backup/recovery method and an 
auditable change record. No production database change may be performed through shared 
credentials. 
• Secure credential transfer and account-activation protocol agreed between the vendor and Liat 
IT before any administrative access is provisioned; authentication secrets are never 
transmitted via email, chat, or meeting minutes. 
Administrative Training & Acceptance Testing 
Designated administrators complete formal training and pass acceptance testing before go -live, 
covering: leave-rule maintenance, accrual-engine and entitlement-ledger updates, role/permission 
management, audit-log review, data-correction procedures, and backup/recovery/controlled-
deletion procedures. Training completion and test results are retained as governance evidence.

any HRIS vendor; it is a Liat design requirement that any evaluated platform must be capable of

7. Staff Travel Automation — Business Requirements 
7.1 Policy Basis 
This section configures the required workflow against the current Staff Travel Policy (Version 3, 
May-26): Section 5 (Business Travel) and Section 6 (Leisure Travel) are treated as two distinct 
request types with two distinct approval chains, because the policy itself treats them as legally and 
operationally different — duty travel is company business with a cost to authorise; leisure travel is 
a discretionary benefit drawn from a fixed annual entitlement. Collapsing the two into one workflow 
would misstate that distinction and is deliberately avoided, on any platform. 
7.2 Registration 
Registration is the platform equivalent of the paper Declaration of Eligible Relatives Form. On hire 
(post-probation, or after three months for contractual staff), HR creates the employee's staff travel 
record; the employee then registers or updates eligible dependants through self-service, subject to 
HR verification against supporting documentation. Registration renews annually on 1 January, with 
an automated reminder rather than a manual tracking exercise.

in Policy §6.3 exactly:

The ledger behaves on the same logic as a standard leave entitlement/accrual engine, repurposed

ticket.

7.6 Commercial's Role — Visibility and Advisory Flag, Not a Veto 
The Staff Travel Policy gives the Chief Commercial Officer approving-officer authority only over 
duty travel originating in Commercial, Sales, Marketing and Customer Experience (§5.3.1(e)). It 
does not give Commercial an approval role over other departments' leisure travel. To avoid the 
platform quietly creating an approval right the policy does not grant, Commercial's role on 
Standby/Space-Available leisure requests is scoped deliberately as visibility plus an advisory flag — 
for example, where a route or date is under commercial pressure for revenue seat inventory — 
attached to the request for HR to weigh, not a hold that blocks the request outright. If Liat intends 
Commercial to hold an actual approval right over space-available leisure travel (as distinct from 
advisory visibility), that is a policy decision for Head of HR and the CCO to make explicitly and 
reflect in a Staff Travel Policy amendment before it is built into the workflow as a hard approval 
gate, on any platform.

11. Acceptance Criteria 
• Each module passes documented UAT with zero open Severity-1 or Severity-2 defects prior to 
go-live. 
• Leave, payroll and travel calculations reconcile to manual/legacy figures at ≥99% accuracy 
across a full test cycle. 
• Performance scores recalculate correctly across quarterly and YTD roll-ups per the approved 
weighting (70% objectives / 30% competencies), with every individual goal traceable to a 
parent corporate objective (Section 6.3–6.4). 
• Every AI-assisted performance record (goal drafting, appraisal summarisation) shows evidence 
of evaluator review and edit prior to sign-off — a wholly unedited AI output is treated as a 
failed acceptance test (Section 6.5). 
• The staff travel entitlement ledger reconciles to policy-defined grade-tier allocations with zero 
unexplained variance at each monthly reconciliation and at year-end reset (Section 7.3, 7.8). 
• Employee benefit registrations and dependant profile creations route directly to the HR 
Management Team dashboard for verification, blocking automated benefit activation until 
explicit HR Management approval is logged. 
• No duty travel booking is shown to have proceeded in the system without both HR policy-
compliance confirmation and Revenue Accounts budget authorisation recorded against the 
request (Section 7.4). 
• Administrative access review evidences named-user accounts, MFA (where supported), and a 
completed segregation-of-duties test with no unresolved conflicts. 
• Selected vendor's Section 5 due-diligence documentation is current, complete and signed off 
by Head of IT prior to Phase 0 close — treated as a gating acceptance criterion, not a post-go-
live formality. 
• Self-service adoption is measurable post go-live against a defined KPI (e.g., monthly active 
users, transaction volume) — the current baseline of ~60–70 monthly logins across ~165 
employees is the pre-implementation reference point. 
• All Phase 1 integrations (payroll, HITIT, CPAT, SharePoint) are confirmed operating end -to-end 
in production, not UAT, before that phase is closed. 
12. Assumptions & Exclusions 
Assumptions 
• Liat will provide timely policy sign-off (vacation, sick leave, staff travel, performance) as the 
authoritative source for configuration, regardless of vendor. 
• The Staff Travel Policy is the authoritative and current version for configuration; any future 
policy amendment (e.g., to Commercial's role on leisure travel — Section 7.6) requires a 
corresponding change request against this framework. 
• Commercial's visibility and flag on leisure travel requests is advisory only, as currently drafted; 
it does not substitute for or override the HR and Finance authorisation required for duty travel.  
• Required data (org hierarchy, approver assignments, entitlement balances, rate tables) will be 
supplied in the format the selected vendor specifies for migration. 
• Feasibility assessments for HITIT and CPAT integration are commissioned and funded as a 
discrete pre-Phase-4 activity.
